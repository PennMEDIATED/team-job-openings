Employment Opportunities with Penn MEDIATED

We are hiring for a Research Director as well as two Knight Postdoctoral Fellows at the Annenberg School for Communications.

Two Knight Postdoctoral Fellows at the Annenberg School for Communications

The Annenberg School for Communications (ASC) of the University of Pennsylvania, in partnership with the Penn Center on Media, Technology, and Democracy (Penn MEDIATED), invites applications for two open postdoctoral researcher positions. Knight Postdoctoral Fellows are intended to be computational social scientists with a background in communication, information or network science, computer science, political science or a relevant discipline. Strong applicants will have expertise and interest in analyzing media content longitudinally and at scale—part of the position will be dedicated to creating durable data infrastructure so that it can persist and be used broadly by the Penn MEDIATED scholarly community. Applications are due by January 10th, 2027. Applicants must apply to work primarily with at least one, and are strongly encouraged to apply to work with two, of the following Penn MEDIATED faculty at the Annenberg School:

Faculty Appointment Research focus
Matthew Levendusky Professor of Political Science and Communication, as well as the Stephen and Mary Baran Chair in the Institutions of Democracy at the Annenberg Public Policy Center; Penn MEDIATED Affiliated Faculty. Candidates should have a substantive interest in studying topics in political communication, including what citizens view as “news” in the age of social media, understanding the role of attention in the current information environment, estimating the effects of novel media content such as podcasts and short-form video, and more. Strong applicants will have analyzed media content at scale: collecting transcript and video content, transcribing audio with speech-to-text models, representing content with embedding models to cluster and track topics, classifying it with fine-tuned or prompted language models validated against human annotation, and linking it to individual-level survey data.
Diana C. Mutz Samuel A. Stouffer Professor of Political Science and Communication; Director of the Institute for the Study of Citizens and Politics (ISCAP); Penn MEDIATED Affiliated Faculty. Professor Mutz studies public opinion, mass political behavior and political communication. The ideal candidate will have skills for analyzing panel data or be willing to acquire them quickly; experience using LLMs in analyzing media content is also valuable. Must enjoy working with other ISCAP affiliates on projects involving U.S. politics and foreign affairs. Background and research interests focused on the study of media, public opinion, electoral politics and democratic accountability will be especially useful.
Danaé Metaxa Raj and Neera Singh Term Assistant Professor in the Computer and Information Science department with a secondary appointment in the Annenberg School for Communications; Penn MEDIATED Faculty Advisor. Dr. Metaxa’s group specializes in human-centered audits and evaluations of AI systems in high-stakes sociopolitical domains; topics include civil rights, elections, and targeted advertising. Ideal candidates will have experience with all phases of the research process, including: (1) problem identification and experimental design, (2) developing and deploying computational infrastructure for data collection, (3) managing large-scale data collection from human subjects, (4) conducting statistical analyses, (5) creation of public-facing web apps to publicize findings, and (6) communication research for academic and general audiences.
Deen Freelon Allan Randall Freelon Sr. Professor and the Presidential Professor at the Annenberg School for Communications; Director of the Politics, Identities, and Communication Lab (PICL); Penn MEDIATED Affiliated Faculty. Professor Freelon performs research at the intersection of social identity, political communication, and computational social science, which centers identity categories such as race, gender, political ideology, religion, as well as sexual, cultural, and national identity. Specific topics of interest include AI representation of social identities; overlapping identity categories in the production and consumption of pro- and anti-democratic media; ingroup-outgroup dynamics in social media conversations; and sustainable and responsible AI- and API-driven research methods.
Dolores Albarracín Penn Integrates Knowledge University Professor; Director of the Social Action Lab; Penn MEDIATED Affiliated Faculty. Professor Albarracín studies the social and psychological processes that shape beliefs, attitudes, communication, and behavior. Her research examines how social environments and communication influence individual psychological processes and action, and how individual behavior can, in turn, affect interpersonal and broader social environments. Areas of particular interest include social influence, belief and attitude formation and change, misinformation, social norms, and behavior. An ideal candidate will combine an interest in social psychological theory with computational methods for studying communication or psychological questions, including natural language processing, large language models, longitudinal data, and social or communication networks. Candidates should have a strong interest in advancing basic social psychological theory and policy questions; candidates whose interests are exclusively applied would not be a good fit.
Duncan Watts Stevens University Professor and a Penn Integrates Knowledge University Professor; Penn MEDIATED Co-Director; Director of the Computational Social Science Lab. Professor Watts seeks to expand research that enhances media transparency and accountability at the scale of the entire information ecosystem using large and highly granular datasets of media consumption and content. An ideal candidate will have experience working with large media datasets and seek to develop monitoring systems for bias and misinformation in media from across the political spectrum and spanning television, radio, social media, and the broader web.

Appointment Details

  • The anticipated compensation is $67,000 per year (or higher based on experience). Penn also provides competitive health, vision, dental, and life insurance policies. Further, Annenberg provides $1,000 for domestic relocation or $2,000 for international relocation; as well as $3,000 per year in research funding.
  • The start date for the position is September 1, 2027.
  • There is no teaching requirement for this position.
  • This postdoc will join a cohort of Knight PhD and Postdoctoral Fellows affiliated with the Penn Center on Media, Technology, and Democracy, attending regular Penn MEDIATED Research Seminars and participating in other Center events and programming.
  • Consistent with the University’s postdoctoral trainee policy, this appointment is for one year, with the possible renewal for a second year, contingent on satisfactory performance and mutual agreement between the fellow and the postdoc’s faculty mentors.
  • The Knight Postdoctoral Fellow is required to work primarily in-person at the University of Pennsylvania’s main campus in Philadelphia.

Qualifications

  • Ph.D. from an accredited university in a relevant field, including Communication, Information or Network Science, Computational Social Science, Computer Science, Political Science, or similar. The Knight Fellow must have successfully defended their dissertation by the start of the Fellowship.
  • A strong research record of peer-reviewed publications or in-progress work on relevant topics and the ability to work independently on research projects.
  • Ability to work as part of a collaborative research team.
  • Expertise in programming, data science, statistics, natural language processing, as well as cloud computing platforms and services.
  • Expertise and experience building data pipelines for reproducible, large-scale research.

Research Director

We are hiring a Research Director to lead a New Data Science Team as we build Penn MEDIATED into a public-facing data-driven research institute for the information ecosystem. See the full job posting and apply on Penn WorkDay.

This is a critical role that will hire and lead a small team (a data engineer and a data visualization specialist) to turn Penn’s cutting-edge computational research and unique media data into visual stories, tools, dashboards, repeated analyses, and other data products that support pro-democracy organizations and outcomes. The goal is to develop meaningful tools and disseminate a better base of knowledge to drive societal and policy change, working with the rest of Penn MEDIATED.

The ideal candidate is an experienced data scientist or computational social scientist, with an interest in media and information technology and a passionate commitment to a healthier U.S. democracy.

Salary up to $125K/year; great Penn benefits; located in Amy Gutmann Hall on Penn’s campus in Philadelphia (Tu/W/Th in-person, M/F remote).
